# Week 1 — Project Exploration

## What I did

This week introduced the Graduation Project and focused on exploring possible directions for an independent Creative Computing project.

The session covered how to define creative computing, distinguish between scientific and artistic project registers, develop a strong project introduction, and evaluate research questions. We then explored possible project ideas through a series of creative provocations before reflecting on our own interests, motivations and potential areas for research.

The final activity asked us to answer five questions designed to identify personal interests, frustrations, technological inspirations and potential audiences. These answers will be used as the starting point for developing a project seed and eventually narrowing down a research direction.

## Question 1 — What makes me angry?

Injustice and the unequal distribution of power make me angry. I am particularly frustrated by how much influence governments, corporations, wealthy individuals and other powerful institutions can have over the lives of ordinary people, while those affected by their decisions can feel powerless in comparison.

I am particularly affected by the genocide in Gaza and the wider situation in Israel and Palestine. It has made me question how political power, wealth, media narratives and access to information influence how people understand major events, whose experiences receive attention, and whose voices are heard. I find it difficult to accept that decisions made by powerful institutions can have such significant consequences for human lives while the people affected by those decisions may have very little influence over them.

This has also made me increasingly interested in the relationship between technology and public opinion. Although people have access to more information than ever, having more information does not necessarily mean having a clearer understanding of events. Digital platforms, news media and political communication can influence which information people encounter, how it is presented, and which perspectives become more visible. This raises questions for me about misinformation, manipulation, transparency and the ability of individuals to critically understand the information they receive.

Ultimately, what makes me angry is the feeling that power is often concentrated within systems that are difficult for ordinary people to see, understand or challenge. This has made me interested in exploring whether creative computing could help make complex information, power structures and competing narratives more transparent, understandable and accessible to people.

## Question 2 — What would I build if I had £1 million and 2 years?

If I had £1 million and two years, I would want to explore how technology and intelligent spatial design could make living in expensive cities more affordable and accessible. I would be interested in developing innovative micro-apartments for cities such as London and New York, where limited space and high housing costs create significant barriers to independent living.

Rather than simply making apartments smaller, I would explore how technology could be used to maximise the functionality of limited living space. This could involve adaptable environments, smart storage, automated systems, modular furniture, or other forms of responsive design that allow one space to serve multiple purposes.

The larger idea interests me because it combines technology, design and a real-world social problem. I would want to explore how computational and spatial design could help create living environments that are smaller in physical size but still functional, comfortable and accessible.

## Question 3 — What piece of technology has blown my mind in the last 12 months?

One of the technologies that has amazed me most over the past year is the rapid development of generative AI, particularly AI-generated images and video. What fascinates me is how quickly the quality has progressed. Only a few years ago, AI-generated video could often be recognised because of obvious visual inconsistencies or unnatural movement. In 2026, increasingly realistic AI-generated content can be much harder to distinguish from authentic footage.
This development has made me think about how quickly our ability to create convincing synthetic media is progressing compared with our ability to identify it. As AI-generated content becomes more realistic, I think it becomes increasingly important for people to know when something they are viewing has been generated or significantly altered by AI.

Rather than expecting people to become experts at identifying AI themselves, technology could provide information about where content came from and how it was created. Systems such as digital watermarks and content credentials are already being developed for this purpose. 
What interests me is the wider question of how we can maintain trust in digital media when seeing something is no longer enough to know that it is real.

## Question 4 — Who is being excluded by today's technology?

I think people with low digital literacy are increasingly being excluded by today's technology. A group I think about particularly are older immigrants who may not be fluent in English, including my own parents. As more essential information, services and communication move online, people who are less familiar with digital technology or who face language barriers can find it much harder to understand, evaluate and navigate what they encounter.
I have noticed that this can also make people more vulnerable to misleading information online. When someone cannot easily verify a source, understand the language being used or recognise how digital content can be manipulated, it can be difficult to distinguish reliable information from misinformation or misleading content.

This concerns me even more as generative AI makes online content increasingly convincing. Someone with strong digital literacy may be more likely to question a suspicious image, video, website or claim, whereas someone who has less experience navigating digital environments may have fewer tools for evaluating it.
This makes me interested in how technology could support people with lower digital literacy and language barriers rather than assuming that every user already understands how digital information works. I think technology should make information easier to understand and evaluate, rather than creating another barrier for the people who need clarity the most.

## Question 5 — What single sentence do I want on my project page?

How can AI be developed and used safely and responsibly in today's world, while ensuring that its rapid development does not outpace humanity's ability to regulate and control its impact, without hindering continued technological advancement and innovation?

## Project Seed

I am interested in exploring how AI can continue to develop and benefit society without progressing beyond our ability to understand, regulate and use it responsibly. I want to investigate how creative computing could help people navigate the risks of rapidly advancing AI, particularly around misinformation, trust, transparency and human decision-making, while still supporting technological innovation.

## Reflection

This exercise helped me realise that my interests are strongly connected to the relationship between technology, information and society. I initially had several different ideas, but working through the questions helped me identify a stronger interest in the rapid development of AI and the challenges that come with it. I am particularly interested in how AI could benefit society while still being developed responsibly and keeping humans informed and in control.
I also realised that I do not yet know exactly what I want to build, and I think that is important at this stage of the project. Rather than deciding on a solution too early, I want to use further research and experimentation to understand the problem first and identify where creative computing could make a meaningful contribution.

My next step is to research existing approaches to AI safety, misinformation, transparency, digital trust and responsible AI, while also looking at creative computing projects that address similar issues. From this, I can begin narrowing my project direction and developing a more specific research question.
