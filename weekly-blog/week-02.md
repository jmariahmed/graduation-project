# Week 2 — Critical Studies: Research and Design for the Graduation Project

## 1. Project Direction

Following Week 1, I have started narrowing my interests in artificial intelligence towards the relationship between transparency, misinformation and human agency. I am particularly interested in how everyday people can evaluate information in an increasingly AI-mediated world without becoming overly dependent on automated decisions.

Rather than committing to a specific product immediately, I want to use academic research and experimentation to identify a meaningful problem and develop a technically ambitious response.

## 2. Project Proposal: Human Agency in an AI-Mediated Information World

Artificial intelligence is increasingly shaping how people encounter, interpret and share information. Generative AI can produce convincing text, images and other media, making it important for individuals to assess the reliability of digital content rather than automatically accepting what they see. However, identifying whether content was generated by AI is not the same as establishing whether it is accurate or presented in the correct context.

This project will investigate how interactive design can help everyday users critically evaluate AI-mediated information, understand uncertainty and retain agency over their judgements. It will explore AI literacy, explainable AI, misinformation and human–computer interaction, particularly in relation to people with varying levels of technical knowledge.

The intended outcome is an interactive digital prototype, potentially developed as a web application or browser extension. One possible technical approach is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which retrieves relevant information from external sources before an AI model generates a response. The system could present supporting evidence, conflicting information and limitations through an accessible interface, encouraging users to investigate claims rather than rely on a definitive AI verdict. The precise functionality will be refined through research.

Existing literature will inform the project's design and evaluation. Almatrafi, Johri and Lee (2024) examine AI literacy, while Laato et al. (2022) investigate how AI systems can communicate their behaviour and outputs to end users. Frau-Meigs (2024) considers media and information literacy in the context of generative AI.

A preliminary research gap to investigate is whether making evidence and uncertainty explorable through an interactive system helps everyday users make better-informed judgements than receiving a conventional AI-generated answer alone. This gap remains provisional and requires further investigation of existing research and products.

Ultimately, the project aims to explore how creative computing can support responsible AI use while preserving human judgement, autonomy and informed decision-making.

## 3. Annotated Bibliography

### Source 1: AI Literacy

**Reference**

Almatrafi, O., Johri, A. and Lee, H. (2024) 'A systematic review of AI literacy conceptualization, constructs, and implementation and assessment efforts (2019–2023)', *Computers and Education Open*, 6, article 100173.

**Annotation**

This systematic review examines research published between 2019 and 2023 to investigate how AI literacy is defined, developed and assessed. The authors identify six key constructs: recognising AI, understanding it, using it, evaluating it, creating with it and navigating it ethically. This source is relevant because it provides a research-based framework for considering what people need to engage critically with AI. It could inform the design and evaluation criteria of my proposed prototype. However, its literature search ended in August 2023, so further research is needed to account for subsequent developments in generative AI.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2024.100173

## 4. Synthesis of Sources

AI literacy research considers the knowledge and skills people need to engage critically with AI, while explainable AI research examines how systems can communicate their behaviour and outputs to users. Media and information literacy extends these concerns to the wider information environment, where people must evaluate sources and retain agency over their decisions. Together, these perspectives motivate investigating whether an interactive system can connect accessible explanations and evidence with users' own evaluation of information.

## 5. Preliminary Research Gap

The gap I intend to investigate is whether making supporting evidence, conflicting information and uncertainty explorable within an interactive AI system helps everyday users make better-informed judgements than receiving a conventional AI-generated answer alone.

This is a provisional research gap rather than a claim that no similar systems exist. Further literature review and analysis of existing verification tools will be necessary to establish what has already been attempted, where limitations remain and how my project could make a distinct contribution.
